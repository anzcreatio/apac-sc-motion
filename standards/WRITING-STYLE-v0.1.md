# Writing style v0.1: sound like an SC, not like AI

**Applies to:** every word a customer sees (slides, one-pagers, emails) and the talk track in the notes.
**Look:** Word documents follow `DOC-STANDARD-v0.1.md` (Creatio brand, doc kit); decks follow `DECK-STANDARD-v0.6.md`.
**Built from:** Renzo's own edits to generated decks (H&G, 10 Nov 2026) and his click-path speaker lines (Suntory,
11 Nov). The builder flags the patterns in §3, but read the whole page: the checker can't hear tone.

## 1. The test

Read the slide out loud as if you're across the table from the customer. If you wouldn't say it that way, rewrite it.

## 2. Rules

| Rule | Instead of | Write |
|---|---|---|
| Plain nouns first, the way people talk | "Every site, its own wish list" | "A wish list for every site" |
| One idea per title, no colon set-ups | "What we heard: your field tools were built for a business you've since outgrown" | "Your field tool was built for one way of selling" |
| Say what they'll see or get | "Our first take: one visit, briefed before and captured after" | "Every visit briefed before and written up after" |
| Short titles: about 8 words, never more than 12 | "Opportunity, quote and sale side by side show the gaps, and gaps become coaching" | "See the gaps and coach the reps" |
| Simple verbs: see, know, chase, change, check | "Empowers reps to unlock insights" | "Reps see what to chase next" |
| Their words, not ours | "Field execution optimisation" | "The right information before the rep walks in" |
| Talk to them: "you", "your reps", "your team" | "The organisation's field force" | "Your reps" |
| Numbers as digits, units kept | "ten things", "three to six months" | "10 things", "3 to 6 months" |
| One sentence per bullet, no semicolons on slides | "Address, job type, builder; the builder rolls up all sites" | "Address, job type and builder on one page. The builder sees all their sites." |
| Say who decides when AI is involved, in plain words | "Human-in-the-loop governance" | "The agent drafts, the rep confirms" |
| Labels above titles are short and plain | "PART 3 · CAPTURED AFTER THE VISIT" | "AFTER THE VISIT" |
| Don't explain the slide on the slide | "This slide shows how…" | (just show it) |

**Good examples (Renzo's click path):** "And notice who is in charge: the agent drafts, a person confirms." · "Two
reps, two ways of operating, one plan." · "Nobody touches the agent, and it behaves differently on the next request."
· "And the manager's number moved because the rep finished a visit."

## 3. Words and patterns to avoid (the builder warns)

- **Vendor words:** seamless, leverage, unlock, empower, robust, streamline(d), cutting-edge, transform(ative),
  holistic, synergy, elevate, harness, game-changer, world-class, best-in-class (unless it's a named analyst rating),
  revolutionise, supercharge, delve, ecosystem, paradigm, single source of truth, at scale, next-generation.
- **AI tells:** em dashes (—) on slides · "not just X, but Y" · "It's not X, it's Y" · stacked triples of adjectives
  · "whether you're X or Y" · "in today's fast-paced world" · rhetorical questions as titles (except the one closing
  question) · the same metaphor twice in one deck.
- **Shape:** a colon in a title · a title over 12 words · a semicolon in slide text.

## 4. Notes (talk track) are different

Notes are for the SC: full sentences, sources and timestamps, cautions. Still plain English, but they can be longer.
Write the "say" lines the way the click path does: short, spoken, one point each.
